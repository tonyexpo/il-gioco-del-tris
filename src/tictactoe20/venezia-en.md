# Venice: A City on the Water

Venice is a city in northeastern Italy, built on a group of islands in the Venetian Lagoon. Its canals, bridges and historic buildings make it unlike most other cities. For centuries, Venice connected Europe with other parts of the Mediterranean through trade, art and ideas.

## A Brief History

Venice grew as people settled on the islands of the lagoon during the centuries following the decline of the Western Roman Empire. Over time, it became the centre of the Venetian Republic, a maritime power governed for centuries by an elected leader known as the doge. Venetian merchants traded across the Mediterranean, bringing wealth and cultural influences to the city.

The republic came to an end in 1797, when Napoleon's forces reached Venice. After periods of French and Austrian rule, Venice became part of the Kingdom of Italy in 1866.

## Life in the Lagoon

In the historic centre, boats take the place of many vehicles used in other cities. Residents and visitors travel by public water bus, known as the *vaporetto*, as well as by private boat or on foot. Gondolas are an enduring symbol of Venice, although they are not the main form of everyday transport for most residents.

The lagoon is more than a setting for the city: it is a delicate environment of water, islands and wetlands. Venice also experiences exceptionally high tides, known as *acqua alta*, which can flood streets and squares. The MOSE system of movable barriers is designed to protect the lagoon from exceptionally high tides, though managing flooding remains a long-term challenge.

## Art and Culture

St Mark's Square is home to St Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace, two landmarks that reflect Venice's religious, political and artistic history. The Rialto Bridge crosses the Grand Canal near an area long associated with commerce. Beyond these famous sites, museums, churches and neighbourhoods preserve works by artists such as Titian and Tintoretto.

Venice is also known for its Carnival, its tradition of glassmaking on the island of Murano and the Venice Biennale, an international cultural exhibition with events devoted to art, architecture and other disciplines.

## A City with a Future

Venice attracts visitors from around the world, but tourism also creates pressure on housing, public space and everyday life. Protecting the lagoon and its buildings while supporting the people who live and work there is essential to the city's future. Venice is not only a place to admire: it is a living city whose history continues to unfold.
